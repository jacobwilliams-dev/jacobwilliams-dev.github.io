# Elevator pitch feedback

### What works

The order is easy to follow. You say who you are, what you build with, what you're like to work with, how fast you learn, and what you want next. I never had to guess where it was going.

The best line is "four different tech stacks in just four months." It's a number, it's true, and it's the one thing I'd still remember an hour later.

Line 2 names real work: REST APIs, secure logins, admin dashboards, a food delivery app. That lands harder than any adjective you could put in front of "developer."

### What doesn't work

The first sentence could belong to anyone. "A passion for building fast, reliable web and mobile apps" is close to what most bootcamp grads say, and it's the line people hear while they decide whether to keep listening.

Five technologies in one sentence is a lot to say in one breath, and more than a listener will hold onto.

"Projects that make people's lives easier" is too vague to mean much. What kind of projects? For who?

The ending doesn't tell anyone where to find you. A recruiter who liked the pitch has nowhere to go next.

I'm honestly not sure what the best new opener is. The food delivery app is concrete, but the Lighthouse 100 scores on CodeBloggs might be more surprising. Try both out loud and keep the one you'd tell a friend first.


### Delivery in the video

Work on word emphasis. Lean harder on the words that matter, like "four different tech stacks in just four months," so the best lines don't get the same weight as everything else.

Work on pauses. Stop for a beat after each main point so it has a second to land before the next one starts.

## What I changed for Script #2 and Video #2

1. Rewrote the opener. I cut "with a passion for" and "actually", and added that I'm a recent CodeBoxx Academy graduate.
2. Grouped the tech into front end (React, React Native) and back end (Node.js, Java Spring Boot), so it's four tools instead of five.
3. Went from three soft skills to two. I kept the eye for detail and made problem solving concrete: I don't let a hard bug go until I find what's really causing it.
4. Reworded "projects that make people's lives easier" to "products that make everyday life easier for people".
5. Ended by naming the role I want, junior developer, and telling people where to find me: my portfolio URL and LinkedIn.
6. Cut 11 words, from 151 to 140, so there's room to pause. The tight deadlines line went, and the list of what I've built got shorter.
